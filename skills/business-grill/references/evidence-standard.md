# Evidence standard

Use this standard to prevent confident stories from outrunning their support.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning | Examples | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| E0 | Unsupported assertion | “Customers need this”; an uncited model answer | Generate a question only |
| E1 | Weak signal | One anecdote, generic market article, stated interest | Form a hypothesis |
| E2 | Direct but limited evidence | Recorded interview, measured quantity, written quote, observed workflow, signed specification | Design a test; provisional estimate |
| E3 | Repeated independent evidence | Several consistent observations, comparable quotes, repeated usage, pilot completion, deposits | Conditional commitment |
| E4 | Behavioral or reconciled proof | Paid renewals, transaction history, audited/reconciled costs, replicated controlled result | Scale or firm commitment, subject to risk gates |

Evidence strength depends on the claim. A vendor quote is direct evidence of what the vendor offered, not proof that every line is necessary or market-priced. A customer saying “interesting” is interview evidence, not willingness-to-pay evidence.

## Evidence ledger

For each decision-critical claim record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Specific, falsifiable statement |
| Type | Fact, reported claim, inference, assumption, or decision |
| Impact | What changes if the claim is false |
| Evidence | Artifact, observation, or result |
| Provenance | Source, author, date, and location/link when available |
| Level | E0–E4 |
| Contrary evidence | The strongest known contradiction or alternative explanation |
| Confidence | Low, medium, or high, with one sentence of reasoning |
| Gap/test | Cheapest ethical way to improve confidence |

## Source discipline

- Prefer primary records: transactions, measurements, signed scopes, product telemetry, interview notes, contracts, invoices, and direct observations.
- Use current, comparable benchmarks. State geography, date, unit, tax treatment, and inclusion/exclusion differences.
- Triangulate consequential claims with more than one independent source where practical.
- Distinguish source freshness from source quality.
- Cite exact file names, sections, rows, pages, or URLs when available.
- Do not treat search snippets, generated summaries, or unattributed screenshots as final evidence.
- Record what could not be accessed. Absence of evidence is not proof of absence.

## Business-hypothesis proof ladder

Prefer behavior over opinion:

1. generic interest;
2. problem interview with a concrete recent example;
3. access to real workflow/data;
4. commitment of time or introduction to the buyer;
5. letter of intent or paid discovery;
6. deposit/pilot payment;
7. repeat use or renewal;
8. profitable delivery without owner heroics.

Do not call an idea validated because people liked a demo.

## Estimate-review proof ladder

For a repair or construction estimate, separate:

1. **Scope necessity:** drawing, defect record, inspection, or user requirement.
2. **Quantity:** measurement sheet and unit conversion.
3. **Unit rate:** material specification, supplier price, labor productivity, equipment rate.
4. **Completeness:** demolition, preparation, delivery, disposal, protection, testing, cleanup, permits, taxes, and exclusions.
5. **Commercial terms:** schedule, payment milestones, warranty, change-order rules, holdback, and acceptance criteria.

An unusual price is a prompt for reconciliation, not evidence of fraud.

## Confidence labels

- **Low:** E0–E1 dominates, decisive contradictions are unresolved, or sources are stale/non-comparable.
- **Medium:** decisive claims have E2–E3 support, but at least one material uncertainty remains.
- **High:** decisive claims have multiple independent E3 signals or E4 proof, contradictions are reconciled, and the result survives sensitivity analysis.
