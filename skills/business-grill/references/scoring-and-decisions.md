# Scoring and decision gates

Use the scorecard after evidence collection. Score each dimension from 0 to 4, multiply by its weight, and divide by 4. Round the total to a whole number.

## Weighted scorecard

| Dimension | Weight | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Problem severity and urgency | 15 | Cosmetic or vague | Real but deferrable | Costly, frequent, time-bound |
| Customer/user evidence | 15 | Assumed | Direct limited evidence | Repeated behavioral evidence |
| Willingness to pay or quantified value | 15 | None | Plausible/one commitment | Paid/repeated/reconciled value |
| Unit economics and cash exposure | 15 | Unknown or structurally negative | Viable under assumptions | Proven margin and bounded downside |
| Delegability and owner independence | 15 | Owner is the system | Partly documented/delegated | Role, SOP, metric, escalation proven |
| Distribution or procurement feasibility | 10 | No credible route | One plausible route | Repeatable route with evidence |
| Delivery feasibility and time-to-learning | 10 | Slow/unclear/high complexity | Testable with effort | Fast bounded test, known capability |
| Risk, reversibility, and trust | 5 | Unacceptable/irreversible | Manageable with controls | Low, reversible, monitored |

Score explanations must cite the evidence level used. Missing evidence normally caps a dimension at 1. A strong narrative does not lift the cap.

## Fatal gates

Check these before using the total. Any unresolved fatal gate blocks GO:

- material legal, safety, privacy, ethical, or licensing risk;
- loss could threaten household or business liquidity;
- decision requires an irreversible commitment before decisive evidence can be obtained;
- economics rely on unpaid owner labor or unrealistic utilization;
- quality depends on one person with no feasible control or replacement;
- key quantities, scope, buyer, payer, or acceptance criteria are undefined;
- data provenance is too weak to support the consequence.

## Decision rules

These are defaults, not mechanical truth. Explain any exception.

### GO

Use when:

- total score is normally 75–100;
- no fatal gate remains;
- decision-critical claims have at least E3 support, or exposure is small and reversible;
- owner load and delegation controls are credible;
- commitment has a budget/time cap and review point.

### VALIDATE

Use when:

- total score is normally 50–74, or one decisive claim remains below E3;
- the uncertainty is resolvable with a bounded ethical test;
- the cost of learning is materially below the cost of being wrong.

Name the exact claim, test, threshold, cap, deadline, and decision that follows each result.

### PIVOT

Use when:

- the problem or asset has credible evidence, but the current segment, offer, channel, price, scope, or delivery model fails;
- changing one or two core choices preserves more evidence than starting over;
- the revised hypothesis is stated clearly enough to test.

Do not label random expansion a pivot.

### STOP

Use when:

- total score is normally below 50 and the core problem or willingness to pay is weak;
- a fatal gate cannot be reduced within the user's risk limit;
- two well-designed tests fail the same decisive hypothesis;
- the idea creates an owner-operated job contrary to the user's stated goal;
- more research is unlikely to change the decision enough to justify its cost.

## Sensitivity check

Before finalizing:

1. Identify the three assumptions with the largest effect on the decision.
2. Recalculate or reason through a downside case for each.
3. Ask whether a 20% cost increase, 20% revenue decrease, or doubled owner time changes the decision.
4. If a small change flips GO to STOP, lower confidence and usually choose VALIDATE.

## Kill criteria quality

Good kill criteria are observable and time-bound:

- fewer than 2 of 10 qualified buyers pay a deposit by a stated date;
- delegated delivery exceeds 6 hours per client for three consecutive clients;
- reconciled estimate remains more than 15% above two scope-equivalent quotes;
- owner involvement does not fall below 4 hours per week after two documented cycles.

Avoid vague criteria such as “traction feels weak” or “costs seem high.”
